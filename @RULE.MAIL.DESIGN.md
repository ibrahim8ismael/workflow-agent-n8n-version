# Woops Email Design System

This document defines the visual, content, and implementation standards for every email sent by Woops. It applies to product emails, workspace communication, operational notifications, and emails sent by AI employees.

The goal is for every message to feel like a clear, useful note from a trusted member of the user's digital workforce: focused, human, and easy to act on.

## 1. Design Principles

- **Useful first**: Every email should have one clear purpose and one primary action.
- **Human, not robotic**: Use direct language and explain what happened, why it matters, and what the recipient can do next.
- **Calm by default**: Use emphasis and color intentionally. Avoid making routine updates feel urgent.
- **Recognizably Woops**: Keep the same logo, colors, typography, spacing, and voice across all templates.
- **Progressive disclosure**: Show the essential information first. Put supporting context, technical details, and secondary actions below it.
- **Accessible everywhere**: Emails must remain understandable without images, color, custom fonts, or a wide screen.

## 2. Email Categories

Each email belongs to one of these categories. The category determines its tone, hierarchy, and allowed actions.

### Transactional and Security

Examples:

- One-time password (OTP)
- Sign-in alerts
- Workspace invitations
- Email address changes
- Security notifications

These emails are functional and time-sensitive. Keep them short, explicit, and free of promotional content.

### Onboarding and Lifecycle

Examples:

- Welcome to Woops
- Workspace setup guidance
- Employee blueprint ready
- Employee deployed successfully
- First activity summary

These emails help users reach value quickly. Explain the next meaningful step instead of listing every available feature.

### Employee Activity and Operations

Examples:

- Daily or weekly activity summaries
- Completed task notifications
- Approval requests
- Escalations requiring a human decision
- Integration or knowledge-base warnings

These emails should make the employee's work legible: what happened, what was affected, and whether the manager needs to respond.

### AI Employee Outbound

Examples:

- Customer support replies
- Sales outreach
- Scheduling messages
- Follow-ups
- Status updates sent on behalf of a business

These emails represent a business and an AI employee. They must follow the workspace's approved identity, signature, permissions, and communication rules. They must never imply human action when the message was generated by an AI employee.

## 3. Brand Foundation

The email system follows the product design tokens in `DESIGN.md` and `src/app/globals.css`.

### Colors

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary blue | `#2563EB` | Primary buttons, links, key actions |
| Deep blue | `#1D4ED8` | Button hover and active link states |
| Soft blue | `#EFF6FF` | Callout backgrounds and selected states |
| Slate 900 | `#0F172A` | Headings and primary text |
| Slate 600 | `#475569` | Body copy and supporting text |
| Slate 400/500 | `#94A3B8` / `#64748B` | Metadata and low-emphasis text |
| Slate 50 | `#F8FAFC` | Email canvas and subtle panels |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Main content surfaces and button text |
| Gray 200 | `#E5E7EB` | Dividers, borders, and input-like surfaces |
| Destructive red | `#EF4444` | Errors and security warnings only |
| Success green | `#16A34A` | Successful completion states only |

Do not use color as the only way to communicate status. Pair status colors with a label, icon, or explanatory text.

### Typography

- Preferred typeface: Figtree.
- Email-safe fallback: `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`.
- Use `font-family` fallbacks in every template. Do not rely on web fonts loading.
- Headings should be bold, concise, and sentence case.
- Body copy should use a comfortable line height of approximately `1.5`.
- Use monospace only for OTP codes, IDs, or technical values that benefit from fixed-width alignment.

### Logo

- Use the Woops logo from `/logo/logo.svg` or the approved hosted email asset.
- Keep the logo on a white or very light background.
- Do not recolor, stretch, rotate, or place the logo over a busy image.
- Include meaningful alternative text such as `Woops` when the logo communicates the sender identity.

## 4. Email Anatomy

Every product email should use this order unless the message type requires a shorter layout.

1. **Preheader**: A short summary visible beside the subject in many inboxes. It should add information rather than repeat the subject.
2. **Brand header**: Woops logo with generous top and side spacing.
3. **Eyebrow or status label**: Optional category label such as `SECURITY`, `EMPLOYEE UPDATE`, or `ACTION NEEDED`.
4. **Headline**: State the outcome or request in one sentence.
5. **Context**: Explain what happened and why the recipient received the email.
6. **Primary action**: Use one descriptive button or prominent code block.
7. **Supporting details**: Include metadata, steps, expiration, or related context in a simple panel.
8. **Secondary action**: Optional text link for a lower-priority action.
9. **Footer**: Sender identity, support contact, legal links, and notification preferences where applicable.

## 5. Layout and Responsive Rules

- Use a centered content container with a maximum width of `600px` to `640px`.
- Use a light neutral outer canvas and a white content surface.
- Use consistent horizontal padding of roughly `24px` on desktop and `20px` on mobile.
- Prefer a single-column layout. Use columns only for simple, non-essential metadata.
- Keep the main content surface visually separate with a subtle border or shadow, but do not depend on shadows for structure.
- Buttons should have a minimum touch target of `44px` in height.
- Keep primary button labels action-oriented: `Verify email`, `Review employee`, `Approve request`, or `Open workspace`.
- Allow headlines and buttons to wrap on small screens rather than forcing horizontal scrolling.
- Keep important information above the first major scroll on mobile.

## 6. Component Standards

### Header

Use a simple Woops logo header. Avoid full navigation bars, multiple links, or application-side navigation inside product emails.

### Primary Button

- Background: `#2563EB`.
- Text: white.
- Border radius: approximately `8px`.
- Font weight: bold or semibold.
- Include a descriptive label and a meaningful destination.
- Provide an equivalent text link when the action is critical and the email client may not render the button correctly.

### Secondary Link

Use a blue text link for supporting actions. Avoid presenting multiple competing buttons.

### Information Panel

Use a lightly tinted panel for details such as:

- Employee name and department
- Workspace name
- Expiration time
- Integration status
- Activity counts

Panels should have a visible heading or label and should not contain dense tables on mobile.

### OTP Code Block

OTP emails must make the code immediately scannable.

- Display the six-digit code as one value with clear spacing.
- Use a large, bold, fixed-width treatment.
- Place the expiration time directly below the code.
- Do not include the code only in an image.
- Do not combine the code with unrelated marketing content.
- Include a security note such as: `If you did not request this code, you can safely ignore this email.`

The current product flow describes the code as a six-digit verification code, so email copy must use the same terminology.

### Status Message

For success, warning, and error states, include all three:

- A short status label
- A plain-language explanation
- The next action or expected resolution

Example: `Connection needs attention` followed by the affected integration and a `Review integration` action.

### Footer

Product emails should include:

- `You are receiving this email because...` context when useful
- Support or contact information
- Workspace or product identity
- Notification settings for configurable notifications
- Unsubscribe controls for non-essential marketing or digest emails
- Privacy and terms links where required

Transactional security emails must not hide essential information behind an unsubscribe link.

## 7. Content and Voice

### Voice

- Clear and confident
- Warm but not overly casual
- Concise and specific
- Helpful without being theatrical
- Honest about AI involvement

### Writing Rules

- Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
- Prefer active voice: `Sarah completed the follow-up` instead of `The follow-up was completed`.
- Lead with the outcome, not internal system details.
- Use the recipient's name only when it is reliable and available.
- Explain unfamiliar terms the first time they appear.
- Avoid excessive exclamation marks, hype, and vague claims.
- Do not expose internal IDs, stack traces, prompts, or tool names unless they help the recipient resolve a problem.
- Use absolute dates and times when a deadline could be ambiguous.

### AI Employee Identity

When an email is sent by an AI employee:

- Use the employee's configured name, role, and approved signature.
- Make the business relationship clear.
- Disclose AI involvement when required by workspace policy, law, or channel expectations.
- Never claim that a human reviewed, approved, or performed an action unless that actually happened.
- Include a human escalation path for sensitive, high-impact, or disputed matters.

## 8. Template Guidance

### OTP and Sign-In

Required content:

- Clear verification headline
- Six-digit code
- Expiration time
- Recipient email or sign-in context when available
- Recovery or security note

Recommended subject format: `Your Woops verification code`

### Welcome and Workspace Invite

Required content:

- Why the recipient is receiving the email
- Workspace or inviter identity
- One primary action
- Invitation expiration when applicable
- Support or security guidance

Recommended subject format: `You have been invited to join {{workspaceName}}`

### Blueprint and Employee Updates

Required content:

- Employee name and role
- Current state: ready, active, paused, or needs attention
- One-sentence summary of the latest change
- Primary review or management action
- Relevant activity or configuration details

Recommended subject format: `{{employeeName}} is ready for review`

### Activity Summaries

Required content:

- Reporting period
- Completed work
- Items requiring attention
- Notable outcomes or failures
- Link to the full activity view

Keep summaries scannable. Use short sections and counts instead of a large unstructured activity log.

### Alerts and Approval Requests

Required content:

- Clear urgency level
- What happened
- What decision or action is needed
- Deadline or impact, if applicable
- Safe destination for taking action

Do not use red for routine warnings. Reserve urgent language for situations that genuinely require immediate human attention.

## 9. Accessibility and Compatibility

- Use semantic text structure with a clear heading hierarchy.
- Maintain sufficient contrast for text, links, and status indicators.
- Include descriptive `alt` text for meaningful images and empty alt text for decorative images.
- Keep the email understandable when images are blocked.
- Never place essential text inside an image.
- Provide plain-text alternatives for every HTML email.
- Use both visible link text and descriptive `title` or accessible labels where supported.
- Avoid relying on hover states, animation, or JavaScript.
- Use email-client-safe HTML and inline critical styles.
- Test in narrow mobile widths and common desktop clients before release.
- Keep the message size reasonable and avoid unnecessary large images.

## 10. Data and Template Conventions

Templates should use explicit, descriptive placeholders. Examples:

```text
{{recipientName}}
{{workspaceName}}
{{employeeName}}
{{employeeRole}}
{{actionUrl}}
{{expiresAt}}
{{supportUrl}}
```

Rules:

- Provide safe fallback copy when optional values are missing.
- Escape user-generated content before rendering it into HTML.
- Keep URLs fully qualified and use HTTPS in production.
- Keep subject lines and preheaders as template data so they can be reviewed independently.
- Ensure every template has both HTML and plain-text content.
- Keep localization in mind: allow headings and buttons to wrap, and do not assume English word lengths.

## 11. Review Checklist

Before shipping an email, confirm:

- The purpose is clear from the subject, preheader, and headline.
- There is one obvious primary action.
- The email uses the current Woops logo and brand colors.
- The content works without images or custom fonts.
- The mobile layout has no horizontal scrolling.
- The CTA destination is valid and safe.
- Dates, times, names, and statuses are correct.
- AI employee identity and disclosure rules are followed.
- Accessibility, plain-text, unsubscribe, and legal requirements are satisfied.
- The email has been tested in the target clients.
